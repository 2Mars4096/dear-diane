## 1. Executive Summary

The global semiconductor industry is entering a structurally higher growth phase driven by AI infrastructure build-out, edge-computing proliferation, and recovering end-market demand. We estimate the total addressable market (TAM) at ~$650B in 2025, with AI datacenter silicon (GPUs, custom ASICs, HBM) contributing the fastest incremental growth.

**Investment Thesis:** We are **Overweight** the sector with a 12-month SOXX price target of 6,800 (implied ~18% upside). The leading-edge supply chain—NVIDIA, TSMC, SK Hynix, ASML, and Applied Materials—offers the best risk/reward, while downstream server OEMs/ODMs (Dell, Supermicro, Foxconn) are levered volume plays. Memory is inflecting after a severe 2023 downturn, with HBM3E supply sold out through 2025.

**Key Catalysts:** (1) AI capex cycle extending from training to inference; (2) TSMC N3E/N2 ramp and CoWoS capacity expansion; (3) China export-control dynamics creating a bifurcated supply chain; (4) Memory pricing recovery and HBM mix shift.

**Risks:** Geopolitical disruption (Taiwan Strait), cyclical inventory correction, China demand decoupling, and aggressive capacity expansion leading to 2026–27 oversupply in non-AI nodes.

## 2. Market Overview & Investment Thesis

### 2.1 TAM and Growth Outlook
The World Semiconductor Trade Statistics (WSTS) projects global semiconductor sales of ~$650B in 2025 (+12% YoY), with 2026 advancing to ~$720B (+11% YoY). AI-related silicon is the primary accelerant: we estimate AI datacenter chips (GPUs, TPUs, custom ASICs, and associated HBM) will grow from ~$75B in 2024 to ~$150B+ by 2026, a ~40% CAGR.

| Segment | 2024E ($B) | 2025E ($B) | 2026E ($B) | CAGR |
|---------|------------|------------|------------|------|
| Logic / Micro | 220 | 245 | 270 | 11% |

### 2.2 Supply-Demand Dynamics
**Leading-edge nodes (≤3nm):** TSMC dominates with >90% share of leading-edge logic. CoWoS (chip-on-wafer-on-substrate) advanced packaging capacity is the binding constraint for AI GPU output; TSMC is expanding CoWoS capacity 2× in 2025 but remains sold out.

**Memory:** HBM3E is supply-constrained through 2025. SK Hynix holds >50% HBM3 share, followed by Samsung (~30%) and Micron (~20%). DRAM spot prices have risen 30–50% from 2023 troughs, with DDR5 and HBM commanding the largest premia.

**Mature nodes (28nm+):** Automotive and industrial demand is recovering gradually. Chinese fab capacity expansion (SMIC, Hua Hong) is pressuring pricing in mature nodes and could create localized oversupply by late 2025.

### 2.3 Geographic & Policy Context
U.S. CHIPS Act ($52B) and analogous EU/Japan/Korea subsidies are reshaping fab geography. Intel is attempting to build a viable foundry business with U.S. and EU anchor capacity. Export controls (U.S. BIS Entity List, October 2023 and subsequent updates) restrict China’s access to leading-edge EUV equipment and certain AI chips, creating a two-track supply chain.
