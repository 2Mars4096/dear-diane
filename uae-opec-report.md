## Date Context

---

## Core Reasons for the Exit

### 1. National Interest & Strategic Flexibility
- The UAE energy ministry stated the move aligns with its **"long-term strategic and economic vision"** and allows greater flexibility to respond to a **"new energy age."**
- Source: Forbes, Guardian, Reuters

### 2. Production Quota Dispute with Saudi Arabia
- The UAE has **chafed at OPEC production caps for years**, pushing to raise quotas to reflect its growing capacity.
- Saudi Arabia, as OPEC’s dominant force, pushed back on higher UAE quotas.
- This tension dates back to at least July 2021 and nearly derailed OPEC+ deals.
- Source: NPR, TASS, Upstox

### 3. Geopolitical Context: Iran War
- The announcement came during a period of **US-Israel war on Iran**, Strait of Hormuz tensions, and a US blockade.
- The UAE criticized fellow Arab states for not doing enough to protect the region, signaling a divergence in Gulf cooperation.
- Source: Al Jazeera, NYT, Guardian

### 4. Political Realignment
- The exit is viewed as a blow to Saudi prestige and positions the UAE closer to the **US/Trump administration**, which has long criticized OPEC.
- Source: Guardian, Forbes

### 5. Market Impact
- The UAE is OPEC’s **third-largest producer**; its exit weakens the cartel’s ability to control supply and prices.
- Independent production could increase global supply and put downward pressure on oil prices.
- Source: Reuters, AP News, CTV News

---

## Bottom Line

The UAE quit OPEC primarily to **free itself from Saudi-led production quotas** and pursue its own national economic and energy strategy, amplified by **regional geopolitical tensions** during the Iran conflict. It is both an economic and political move.
