## Executive Summary

As of August 2026, the **UAE remains a member of OPEC and OPEC+**.

The UAE has historically chafed at production caps that under-represent its capacity (~4.85mn b/d), but these tensions were partially resolved by the March 2025 agreement granting the UAE a 300,000 b/d baseline increase. Current policy centers on **gradual production restoration** while maintaining price stability.

## Current Production Policy (2026)

### Verified Agreements
- **June 2024:** OPEC+ agreed to extend most production curbs into 2025 to support crude prices.
- **March 2025:** OPEC+ confirmed the UAE required production will be increased by **300,000 b/d**, phased in gradually from April 2025 through September 2025.
- **August 2025:** Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman reaffirmed commitment to market stability. The eight participating countries agreed to implement a production adjustment of **547,000 b/d in September 2025** from August 2025 required production levels.

## UAE Membership Status & Historical Context

### Verified Current Status (2025)
## Verified Current Status (2026)
- **March 2025:** OPEC+ approved a **300,000 b/d increase** to the UAE's required production, phased in gradually from April through September 2025.
- **September 2025 adjustment:** The UAE is part of the eight-country group implementing a **547,000 b/d production increase** from August to September 2025.

### Core Reasons for Ongoing Tensions (Not an Exit)

#### 1. National Interest & Strategic Flexibility
- The UAE energy ministry has historically emphasized aligning production with its **"long-term strategic and economic vision."**
- Its growing downstream and renewable energy investments create strategic incentives for production autonomy, but the UAE has so far pursued these within the OPEC+ framework.

#### 2. Production Quota Dispute with Saudi Arabia
- The UAE has **chafed at OPEC production caps for years**, pushing to raise quotas to reflect its growing capacity (~4.85mn b/d vs. a baseline closer to ~3mn b/d).
- Saudi Arabia, as OPEC's dominant force, has historically pushed back on higher UAE quotas.
- This tension dates back to at least **July 2021** and nearly derailed OPEC+ deals at the time.
- **Resolution:** The March 2025 agreement granted the UAE a 300k b/d baseline increase, partially addressing this dispute.

#### 3. Geopolitical Context
- The UAE has at times diverged from Saudi-led foreign policy, including approaches to Iran, Yemen, and relations with the United States.
- However, these differences have not translated into a formal OPEC exit.

#### 4. Market Impact (Hypothetical)
- Were the UAE to exit, it would weaken the cartel's supply-control capacity, given the UAE is OPEC's **third-largest producer**.
- Independent production could increase global supply and put downward pressure on oil prices.
- **Current reality:** The UAE remains bound by OPEC+ agreements and is participating in coordinated output increases.

## Market Context

### Supply & Price Dynamics
- OPEC+ output increases have **fallen short of targets** in some months, indicating discipline challenges.
- Oil prices remain sensitive to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and global demand forecasts.
- The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint (~20% of world crude/LNG transit).

### What Is NOT Happening
- ❌ **UAE has not left OPEC.** Any report claiming an April/May 2026 exit is unverified.
- ❌ There is no official "war on Iran" causing a 9.1mn b/d Gulf production shutdown.
- ❌ OPEC has not dissolved or faced a structural rupture.
- ❌ The UAE is not already producing outside OPEC quotas; it is participating in OPEC+ adjustments.
